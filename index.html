Why this exists

There's something you haven't said out loud yet.

The person who could help is forty feet down the hall.

The problem isn't the words. It's not knowing what happens next.

Here's the thing

Those questions have real answers.

Will they call my parents? Will they freak out? What if I can't take it back? Most of what you tell a school adult stays between you. The short list of things they'd have to pass on is actually written in law — and you're allowed to see it before you decide anything. Nobody ever shows it to you, though. So we did.

How it works

Three levels.
You climb at your own speed.

Level 1

Ask

Wondering "what happens if I tell them about…?" Ask here first. No name, nothing saved, and every answer shows you the actual Ontario rule it comes from.

Costs you: nothing. Not even a name.
Level 2

Codename

Write to an adult at your school as "Blue Heron 41" instead of you. It lands in their inbox, they can write back, and their reply comes to your codename. Your name stays yours until you choose to tell them. School addresses only.

Costs you: almost nothing.
Level 3

Tell — on your terms

When you're ready: set the rules for the conversation, find your first sentence, and hand the adult a page that teaches them how to listen — before you say a single word.

Costs you: the real step — taken your way.

The other half

We get the adult ready, too.

Adults mean well, but sometimes they panic, interrupt, or make it a bigger thing than you wanted. So before you talk, they get a page.

Your rules, their instructions

Every request you pick appears on their page.

Ask before any call home. Don't tell my brother. Just listen today. You choose them; the page teaches them.

And the part that stays yours

What you're going to say is never in the link.

The page carries your requests — not your story. That stays with you until the moment you say it out loud.

Trust is built on what we won't do

What this site refuses to do

✕
No accounts, no tracking, no profile of you.

Levels 1 and 3 run entirely in your browser and make zero requests to anyone. Level 2 sends exactly one thing of yours — the message you wrote, to the school address you chose — and stores it under a codename with no name, email, or device attached. (Opening its write screen also pings our relay awake so sending isn't slow; that ping carries nothing you typed.)

✕
Never sends anything you didn't write and press send on.

No auto-alerts, no "we noticed you seem sad" emails to anyone. Every message, every step is a button only you press.

✕
Won't message anyone except a school adult.

Level 2 only delivers to school accounts — never a personal inbox. Without that rule this would be a way to send anonymous messages to anybody, which helps nobody.

✕
Never promises secrecy an adult can't keep.

Ontario law requires adults to report abuse and danger. We show you that line honestly instead of letting you be ambushed by it.

✕
Doesn't counsel, diagnose, or pretend to be a therapist.

It prepares a conversation with a real human. In some places AI "therapy" is now illegal — for good reason.

✕
Doesn't guess.

Answers come from a fixed set of verified Ontario sources, each one cited. If it doesn't know, it says so and points you to a human who does.

✕
Doesn't handle a crisis itself.

If what you type sounds like right now is the emergency, the site steps aside and connects you to real people, immediately.

Fair questions

Asked and answered

Is this private? Really?

There are no accounts and no tracking, anywhere. Levels 1 and 3 — the questions and the terms builder — run entirely inside your browser and make zero external requests: nothing you type there is transmitted anywhere, ever. Drafts live only on your own device, and one button deletes all of it.

Level 2 is the exception, and we'd rather say so plainly than bury it: to put a message in a counsellor's inbox, something has to send it. So that one message goes to a relay we run, which emails it to the school address you picked and keeps the conversation under your codename. What it saves is the message and the codename — no name, no email address, nothing that points back to you. Your IP reaches the relay the way it reaches every website you visit; it is never written down or attached to your conversation. And it only ever writes to school accounts, never a personal inbox.

Honest limits: your browser keeps history like it does for any site; the adult-page link — which contains your requests, never your story — can sit in chat logs; and an email, once it's in someone's inbox, is theirs. On a shared or monitored computer, a private/incognito window plus closing the tab is safest. We tell you this because pretending otherwise would be a lie.

Prove it doesn't send what I type anywhere.

Fair ask — here's a test you can run yourself. After your first visit, this site saves a copy of itself on your device, so Levels 1 and 3 work with the internet fully off: turn on airplane mode, reload, ask a question, build your terms — everything still runs. A tool that needed to send your answers to a server couldn't do that. (Run the test in a normal window — private windows don't keep the offline copy, which is the same reason they're safer on shared devices.)

You can also open your browser's network inspector while you use it: outside Level 2 (which says up front that delivering an email needs a network), the only requests you'll see are the site loading its own files.

Why should I trust the answers?

Because they're not generated — they're retrieved from a fixed set of sources (Ontario's child-protection law, the Ontario College of Teachers' guidance, the provincial privacy commissioner, Kids Help Phone), and every answer links its source. When your question isn't covered, it says "I don't know" and points you to a human. A tool for this subject should refuse to guess.

What if I message someone and they never reply?

Honestly: codename messages ride ordinary school email, and a school's spam filter can quarantine mail from a sender it doesn't recognize — meaning the adult may never have seen it at all. Some boards go further and block all outside senders, in which case Level 2 simply can't reach them until the school adds us as a partner. Silence after a few school days more likely means unseen than ignored.

The ways around a filter: message a different adult, or use Level 3 — it involves no email at all and works everywhere.

Doesn't showing the rules teach kids to strategically avoid reporting?

Research on disclosure says the opposite: what stops young people from telling anyone is uncertainty, not information. People who know exactly what happens next tell more, not less — and the ones who fear an ambush stay silent entirely. Informed consent isn't a loophole; it's the reason to speak at all.

What if I'm not in Ontario?

The legal specifics shown are Ontario's. The listening guidance is universal, but reporting rules differ by province and country — the site says this on every answer rather than letting you assume.

Is this a crisis service?

No — and it knows it. If what you type suggests you're in danger right now, the site stops and connects you to Kids Help Phone (1-800-668-6868, text CONNECT to 686868) and 9-8-8. Those are real people, 24/7, and they're better at this than any website.
